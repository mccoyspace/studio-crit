A Place to Hide (2026)
Digital sculpture concept, found stone, imagined architecture, light.

Artist Statement

A stone is something we find. A home is something we make. But what happens when one appears to have grown inside the other?

A Place to Hide presents a rock containing an intricate architecture of staircases, rooms, and passageways. The exterior suggests geological time; the interior suggests human intention. Yet the stairs lead nowhere definite, and the rooms seem to have been waiting long before anyone arrived.

The work exists as a digitally constructed image of a seemingly physical object. Even the evidence of its existence is manufactured.

I want the viewer to wonder not only which parts of the stone were made or found, but whether the distinction matters once an object becomes believable.
