Instructions for Disappearance

Take a sheet of white paper.

Draw a circle in the center.

Inside it, write the word beautiful.

Look at it until the word becomes a shape.

Erase the word.

Look at the circle until the absence becomes beautiful.

Erase the circle.

Give the blank page to someone else.

Do not tell them what was there.

Artist Statement

Instructions for Disappearance treats aesthetic content as something that can be given, withdrawn, and generated by withdrawal itself. The circle frames an ordinary word as an object of contemplation. Erasure removes its visible content but leaves the viewer with a memory, turning absence into a new aesthetic experience. The final blank page appears to contain nothing, yet the person who performed the instructions sees something different from the person who receives it. The work asks whether beauty belongs to the object, the act of attention, or the knowledge of what has disappeared. Aesthetic content can be taken from a surface, but perhaps not from the experience of having seen it.
